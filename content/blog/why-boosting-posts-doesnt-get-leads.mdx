If you have ever put ₹2,000 behind a post, watched the likes climb, and then realised nobody enquired, you have already met the core problem. The Boost button worked exactly as designed. It just was not designed to get you customers.

## What Boost actually optimises for

Every Meta campaign has an objective, and the objective decides who sees your ad. When you boost from the app, the objective defaults to engagement or profile visits. Meta then goes and finds the people most likely to like, comment or tap — because that is what you asked for.

Those people exist in large numbers and they are cheap to reach. They are also, largely, people who like things and then move on. Your ₹2,000 bought a real outcome; it was just the wrong one.

A lead campaign built in Ads Manager sets the objective to leads or conversions. Now Meta goes looking for a different group entirely: people who have historically filled in forms and messaged businesses. Fewer people, more expensive to reach, and vastly more likely to become a customer.

## The same budget, three different outcomes

Take ₹20,000 in a mid-size Indian city, advertising a gym trial pass.

- **Boosted post, engagement objective.** Perhaps 60,000 people reached, 900 likes, a handful of DMs. Cost per like around ₹22. Cost per enquiry: unknowable, because nothing was tracked.
- **Lead campaign, no offer.** Maybe 12,000 reached and 40 form fills at ₹500 each, most of them people who cannot say what they signed up for.
- **Lead campaign with a trial-pass offer and a two-question form.** Perhaps 10,000 reached, 130 enquiries at around ₹150, most of whom know exactly what they asked for.

The third one costs the same as the first. The difference is entirely in the objective and the offer.

## Why the Boost button still exists

Because it works beautifully for what it is: making a post travel further. If you have a genuinely good piece of content and you want more people to see it, boosting is a reasonable thing to do. Brand awareness is real.

It just is not lead generation, and the app does nothing to tell you that. The button is one tap away inside an interface you already know, and Ads Manager is intimidating. That is a design decision, not a recommendation.

## What to do this week

1. Stop boosting for a fortnight. You will not lose customers you were getting.
2. Open Ads Manager and create one campaign with the leads objective.
3. Write an actual offer. Not "visit us" — a trial, a consultation, a first-visit price.
4. Ask two qualifying questions on the form. Locality and one more.
5. Route the submissions somewhere you will see them within minutes.

If after a month your cost per enquiry is somewhere sane for your category and your team is calling people back the same day, you have a system. If not, at least you now have numbers to argue with, which you never got from the likes.
