This is the first question almost everyone asks, and it is very nearly the wrong one. But it deserves a real answer, so here are the ranges we actually see, followed by the reason you should not lean on them too hard.

## The ranges

These are typical costs per enquiry for lead campaigns in Indian cities. Wide on purpose.

- **Restaurants and cafés** — ₹40 to ₹150
- **Salons and spas** — ₹60 to ₹200
- **Gyms and fitness studios** — ₹80 to ₹250
- **Coaching institutes** — ₹100 to ₹400
- **Clinics, dental and derma** — ₹150 to ₹600
- **Real estate** — ₹300 to ₹1,500

Where you land inside a range depends on your city, how many competitors are bidding for the same attention, how good your creative is, and above all how compelling your offer is. A metro is more expensive than a tier-two city, often by half again.

## Why the number on its own is close to meaningless

A dental clinic paying ₹200 per enquiry sounds like it is doing better than one paying ₹500. Now add the missing column.

The first clinic gets enquiries from a discount-led ad. Twenty per cent answer the phone, and one in ten of those books. That is fifty enquiries to get one patient: ₹10,000 per patient.

The second leads with a paid consultation. Seventy per cent answer, and four in ten book. That is about three and a half enquiries per patient: ₹1,750 per patient.

The clinic with the "expensive" leads is acquiring patients at a fifth of the cost. Cost per enquiry told you the opposite.

## The number that actually matters

Cost per customer. Take what you spent, divide by the customers it produced. That single figure absorbs your close rate, your follow-up speed and your lead quality all at once, which is exactly why agencies prefer to report cost per lead instead.

You can work yours out in about a minute with [the calculator](/tools/cpl-calculator).

## When cost per lead is worth watching

It is a useful early-warning signal, not a scorecard.

- **It doubles in a week.** Usually creative fatigue — the same people are seeing the ad too often.
- **It is far below your range.** Often a sign the form is too easy or the offer is too vague, and you are collecting people who do not know what they clicked.
- **It rises as you scale.** Normal and expected. You have exhausted the cheapest audience and are reaching further out.

## What to do with all this

Use the ranges to sanity-check a proposal, not to choose an agency. If someone quotes you a precise number before asking what you sell, at what price, in which city, against whom — they are quoting an average, and averages do not run your business.

And be suspicious of anyone promising a cost per lead in writing. It depends on your offer and your follow-up as much as on the media buying, and [we will not guarantee it either](/pricing).
